Meta Andromeda & modern ad delivery

A digital marketer’s field guide to how Meta narrows, predicts, ranks and delivers ads—and what to change in creative, audience, campaign structure, signals and measurement.

01
Start here

Executive summary

Andromeda is a retrieval system inside Meta’s advertising recommendation stack. It helps select a small, personalized set of eligible ads from a very large pool so later, more expensive ranking and auction steps can make the final delivery decision. It is important—but it is not the whole Meta algorithm, the auction, or an advertiser-facing campaign setting. [1][4]

01 · What it does

Finds plausible ads early

Meta says Andromeda’s retrieval stage reduces tens of millions of ad candidates to a few thousand relevant candidates for later ranking. [1]

02 · Why now

More candidates, little time

Automation and generative creative expand the eligible ad pool. Meta’s engineering challenge is to personalize at scale without violating strict serving-latency and infrastructure limits. [1]

03 · What changed

Better retrieval, not “AI appeared”

Machine learning already informed Meta ad prediction and auctions years before Andromeda. The specific change is a stronger, scalable retrieval stage within a multi-stage system. [3][1]

04 · Practical shift

Give the system useful options

Move from endlessly splitting similar audiences toward a sound objective, clean event feedback and a manageable portfolio of meaningfully different creative concepts. Interpretation

05 · Keep perspective

Targeting did not disappear

Location, exclusions, audience controls, policy and campaign eligibility still matter. In Advantage+ audience, Meta distinguishes hard controls from suggestions that may expand. [6]

06 · Measurement

Bad inputs remain bad inputs

Andromeda cannot turn a misfiring Purchase event or low-quality lead into a truthful business outcome. Audit the Pixel/CAPI and CRM feedback before blaming delivery. [14][16]

Meta-reported result, read narrowly: Meta’s Dec 2024 engineering post reported a 6% improvement in retrieval recall and an 8% improvement in ad quality on selected segments. These are Meta-reported system results—not a universal 6%/8% advertiser lift in ROAS, CTR, CPA or conversion rate. [1]

Stop treating the account like a targeting maze

  • Stop cloning ad sets just to isolate every interest.
  • Stop changing budgets, audiences and creative all at once.
  • Stop reading an attribution column as proof of causation.
  • Stop assuming more ads automatically means more learning.

Start managing a signal-and-creative system

  • Start with the business outcome and a valid optimization event.
  • Start with broad-but-eligible reach where the offer can serve broadly.
  • Build distinct concepts for different motivations, not cosmetic duplicates.
  • Validate tracking and compare platform results with business outcomes.
Analogy: Imagine a city library with millions of books and a librarian who has only an instant to make a shortlist for one visitor. Andromeda is the fast catalog-and-retrieval desk: it finds a small shelf of plausible books. Other recommendation models compare the shortlist, and the ad auction weighs advertiser bids, predicted action and quality before an ad is delivered. The librarian is not the whole library, the final decision or the reader’s reaction.
How to read the evidence in this guide
Public Meta Engineering articles explain named infrastructure, but they do not expose all production implementation details. Meta Help Center pages describe advertiser-facing product behavior and may change as tools roll out. Meta-reported performance outcomes are self-reported and scoped to the stated test, product, population or segment. The marketer recommendations in this report are labeled interpretation when they go beyond an official product description.

What marketers controlled more directly

  • Demographics and interests: age, location, interests and behaviors, subject to policy and product availability.
  • Lookalikes: a first-party or engagement seed used to find people similar to that source.
  • Custom audiences and retargeting: known customers, website visitors, app users or social engagers.
  • Ad-set structure: separate audience tests, budgets, placements or funnel messages.
  • Manual allocation: shift budget toward an audience/ad-set cell that appears to be performing.

Why segmentation can fail

  • Similar ad sets can overlap and compete for many of the same people.
  • Splitting spend spreads conversions across more learning units.
  • Small samples make apparent “winners” noisy; many simultaneous tests raise false-winner risk.
  • Interest labels are imperfect proxies for purchase intent.
  • Every extra cell requires more creative, tracking, QA and decision-making.
Meta-documented Meta’s public 2020 explanation already described ads moving from advertiser-selected target audiences into an auction, with machine-learning predictions of an individual’s action likelihood and ad quality. The fair comparison is therefore manual advertiser controls vs. increasingly capable automation—not “manual targeting before AI, AI after Andromeda.” [3]
03
A gradual transition

What changed before Andromeda

The direction of travel was already toward more automated, more sequence-aware prediction. Andromeda sits on that path as a retrieval innovation; it is not a synonym for every Advantage+ product or every AI model.

  1. EARLIER SELF-SERVE ERA
    Audience tools put knobs in the marketer’s hands

    Interest, demographic, custom-audience and lookalike inputs helped define who could be considered. A common tactic was to make one ad set per segment. [3]

  2. 2020 · PUBLIC EXPLANATION
    ML is already in delivery

    Meta publicly describes predicted action rates and ad-quality assessment as inputs to ad delivery and its auction. [3]

  3. 2022 · ADVANTAGE+
    More campaign automation

    Meta introduces Advantage+ Shopping Campaigns globally, describing AI/ML automation of campaign creation and many creative combinations. The product suite evolves separately from the underlying recommender stack. [8]

  4. 19 NOV 2024 · SEQUENCE LEARNING
    Learn from ordered behavior

    Meta Engineering describes moving beyond aggregated, hand-engineered features toward event-based sequence learning for personalized ad recommendations. [2]

  5. 2 DEC 2024 · ANDROMEDA
    Rebuild the retrieval stage

    Meta names Andromeda as its personalized ad-retrieval engine within a multi-stage recommendation system. [1]

  6. 2025 · GEM
    Foundation-model learning spreads

    Meta describes GEM as a recommendation foundation model that transfers learnings across downstream ads models; a separate Meta for Business overview places GEM among multiple AI innovations in ad ranking. GEM is not Andromeda. [22][23]

  7. MAR 2026 · ADAPTIVE RANKING
    More model capacity at serving time

    Meta describes a separate Adaptive Ranking Model focused on efficient, low-latency ranking. Meta reported scoped conversion/CTR lifts for its Instagram launch; these are not universal advertiser guarantees. [24]

  8. JUL 2026 · HIR RESEARCH
    New representation research

    Meta presents Hierarchical Interest Representation as a research area intended to support broader recommendation components, including Andromeda and ranking systems. “Intended to” is not proof of universal rollout. [25]

Keep the product names separate: Advantage+ is an advertiser-facing suite of automation features/campaigns; Andromeda is a Meta Engineering name for ad retrieval; GEM and Adaptive Ranking Model describe other parts of a broader recommendation ecosystem. An account’s exact product controls and rollout can vary. [1][23][24]
04
The name behind the headlines

What is Meta Andromeda?

Simple explanation: Andromeda is an AI-powered “shortlist maker” for Meta ads. When there are vastly more eligible ads than can be deeply evaluated in real time, it retrieves a smaller set that appears relevant to a particular ad opportunity. Other models and the auction continue the work.
Technical explanation: Meta calls Andromeda a proprietary machine-learning system design for retrieval in ad recommendation. It uses a hierarchical ad index and a customized neural network to narrow an extremely large candidate set; larger ranking models then predict people and advertiser value to determine the final ads shown. [1]

Meta describes retrieval as the first stage of a multi-stage ads recommendation system: tens of millions of candidate ads are narrowed to a few thousand; later, larger and more sophisticated ranking models predict value and help determine the final set. The exact stage boundaries are internal and may evolve. [1]

Why retrieval matters at Meta’s scale

A recommender cannot run its most expensive prediction on every ad in the entire marketplace for every impression. Retrieval makes the later work feasible by spending computation on a relevant shortlist. Meta’s Andromeda post describes a hierarchical index that focuses inference on more promising branches, along with hardware/software co-design to meet latency and capacity constraints. [1]

What Meta has disclosed

Andromeda is retrieval; the multi-stage funnel; millions-to-thousands scale; hierarchical indexing; a custom neural network; hardware co-design; selected reported recall/quality improvements. [1]

What is not public

Production feature list, model weights, exact ranking score, thresholds, per-user routing, creative-encoding details, complete roll-out map or how every advertiser’s ads are treated.

What it is not

Not a campaign objective, not a “creative-only” targeting switch, not the final auction, and not the organic Feed recommendation algorithm. Similar recommender ideas can be used in different product systems, but the goals and eligible content differ.

Useful analogy: the ad-recommendation library
Picture millions of catalog cards. The retrieval desk quickly identifies a few thousand plausible books for one reader and one moment; the ranking desk compares those options more carefully; the auction chooses among eligible ads using advertiser and user-value considerations; then the app renders an impression. If the reader clicks, hides, scrolls or later converts—and if those signals are available and permitted—the system can use feedback in later predictions. This is an explanatory analogy, not a literal system diagram.

06
No data-science degree required

Machine-learning concepts in simple terms

Use the toggle above to switch these cards between a plain-language view and a more technical explanation. The examples are marketing analogies, not statements about Meta’s undisclosed model code.

Prediction

Simple: A forecast. Technical: A model estimates the chance or value of an outcome given inputs. Marketing: How likely this impression is to lead to a purchase.
Technical: An inference-time estimate such as a conditional probability or expected value. Marketing: A purchase-probability estimate for a candidate ad/person pair; exact inputs and calibration are proprietary.

Classification

Simple: Sort something into a category. Technical: A supervised model predicts a label or class. Marketing: A lead may be classified as qualified/unqualified once CRM data arrives.
Technical: A model maps features to one or more labels, often by estimating class probabilities. Marketing: Distinguishing a booked consultation from a raw form submission; delivery models may also use continuous probabilities instead of binary labels.

Ranking

Simple: Put options in a useful order. Technical: Score and sort candidate items by predicted relevance/value. Marketing: Order shortlisted ads for possible delivery.
Technical: A ranker compares many candidate items using context-dependent scores or pairwise/listwise objectives. Marketing: A later model can rank a retrieved shortlist; “ranked high” does not guarantee an impression.

Recommendation system

Simple: Software that suggests what to show next. Technical: Candidate retrieval plus prediction/ranking and business constraints. Marketing: Selecting a relevant ad from many eligible campaigns.
Technical: A multi-stage recommender often separates scalable retrieval from computationally expensive ranking. Marketing: Meta’s public Andromeda description explicitly puts it in the ads recommendation retrieval stage.

Embeddings

Simple: A compact numeric “meaning map.” Technical: A vector representation in which useful relationships can be learned. Marketing: Products or event types can be represented so related items are easier to connect.
Technical: Learned vectors encode entities/events so distance or interaction in a latent space carries useful structure. Marketing: Meta Engineering describes event embeddings and precomputed ad embeddings; the actual representation is proprietary.

Retrieval

Simple: Find a manageable shortlist quickly. Technical: Select a high-recall subset from an enormous candidate corpus. Marketing: Andromeda narrows ad candidates before more detailed ranking.
Technical: A low-latency candidate-selection operation, often indexed or hierarchical. Marketing: Retrieval is the stage Meta associates with Andromeda—not the final selection or conversion prediction by itself.

Candidate generation

Simple: Decide which options deserve a closer look. Technical: Produce a compact set of eligible/relevant items; in industry usage this may overlap with “retrieval.” Marketing: Ads inside the valid campaign/audience pool can become candidates.
Technical: The upstream recommender step that reduces search space for downstream scoring. Marketing: Candidate generation and retrieval are sometimes used interchangeably; Meta’s post calls Andromeda retrieval and describes tens of millions → thousands.

Features / signals

Simple: Clues the model can use. Technical: Structured inputs derived from user, ad, advertiser or context data. Marketing: A chosen conversion event, ad format, prior interaction or placement context.
Technical: Features are representations/variables; signals are the observations or evidence feeding a model. The words overlap in practice. Marketing: Accurate Purchase events are more useful than a button-click event falsely labeled Purchase.

Model training

Simple: Learn patterns from examples. Technical: Fit parameters on historical examples and outcomes. Marketing: A delivered ad and valid event history can provide experience for future predictions.
Technical: An offline/online learning process estimates parameters from labeled or self-supervised data; training is distinct from serving an individual request. Marketing: More clean events can help provide evidence, but no universal event count guarantees success.

Inference

Simple: Use what the model learned to make a live estimate. Technical: Run a trained model on current inputs. Marketing: Estimate which ad may suit a person at an impression opportunity.
Technical: Forward computation at serve time under strict latency/capacity constraints. Marketing: Andromeda’s inference is engineered to retrieve candidates quickly; the exact request path is not public.

Feedback loop

Simple: Observe what happened and improve the next decision. Technical: New interaction/outcome data informs later training or delivery estimates. Marketing: A valid, deduplicated purchase event can teach more than an unverified thank-you-page view.
Technical: A data-to-model loop connecting predictions, logged outcomes and future model updates. Marketing: Feedback is only as trustworthy as instrumentation, match quality, event meaning and permitted data use.

Exploration vs. exploitation

Simple: Try promising new options vs. keep using what already works. Technical: A general recommender trade-off under uncertainty. Marketing: A new concept may receive a small test before a proven ad gets most delivery.
Technical: Exploration gathers information; exploitation favors options with known predicted payoff. Marketing: This is a useful general lens for delivery and experiments—not a disclosed Andromeda policy or a control marketers can set directly.
Plain / technical toggle: The global button switches the wording in these concept cards and the Andromeda explainer. “Technical” is still intentionally non-mathematical.

Meta-documented examples

  • Objective-specific estimated action rate; Meta’s public 2020 article names on- and off-platform behavior shared through business tools, content of the ad, time of day and interactions. [3]
  • Ad quality can reflect people viewing or hiding an ad and assessments of low-quality attributes. [3][4]
  • Advantage+ audience help names past conversions, Pixel data and previous ad interactions as examples. [5]
  • Meta’s 2025 GEM article says its model is trained on ad content and user engagement data, with sequence and non-sequence features. [23]

What not to infer

  • Do not claim Meta “reads the ad like a human” or that a particular word/face/color triggers a known score.
  • Do not assume every click, site visit or CRM field is available to every model or account.
  • Do not invent a universal ranking formula, weights, thresholds or real-time logic.
  • Do not confuse a creative response with a business conversion; CTR is not purchase intent by itself.
Reasoned inference When an advertiser supplies multiple genuinely distinct concepts, those ads give a retrieval/ranking system more varied advertiser-approved options to consider. It is reasonable to expect different creative messages to fit different needs, but Meta has not publicly confirmed the exact creative features or matching logic used by Andromeda for each advertiser.

09
An operating shift, not a law

From audience-centric to creative-and-signal-centric marketing

This phrase is a marketer interpretation, not a formal Meta product rule. It means audience settings are less often the only or best optimization lever when automated delivery can use a wider eligible pool and a larger creative library. It does not mean creative is literally the only targeting mechanism.

Before · build the audience map

  1. Write a persona for each audience segment.
  2. Create separate interest/lookalike/custom-audience ad sets.
  3. Attach a tailored message to each ad set.
  4. Move budget among the resulting audience cells.
  5. Read segment ROAS as if it were a clean audience test.

Now · build the signal and creative system

  1. Set one real objective and the correct, measurable conversion event.
  2. Define only the necessary eligibility constraints and audience controls.
  3. Supply different creative concepts for different motivations and moments.
  4. Let eligible delivery test person–ad fits where automation permits.
  5. Evaluate CRM/business outcomes and use experiments for causal questions.

Why broad audiences can help

A wider pool can give a system more opportunities to locate converters, especially when conversion feedback is reliable and the product can serve broadly. Meta describes Advantage+ audience as learning from signals such as past conversions, Pixel data and previous ad interactions. [5]

Broad is a hypothesis to test—not an always-win rule. A broad audience cannot repair poor economics or substitute for a regulated/operational boundary.

Where manual segmentation still helps

Separate materially different offers, purchase paths, languages/geographies, budgets, customer lifecycle strategies, optimization events or policy constraints. Use a distinct audience cell when the test question requires a controlled comparison—not just because an interest exists.

When account UI offers “audience suggestions,” remember Meta Help says suggestions may expand beyond the suggested audience unless tightened as controls. [6]

Practical translation Audience strategy still answers “where may delivery go, and what must be true?” Creative strategy increasingly answers “why should this person care?” Tracking answers “what business result actually happened?” A robust campaign joins all three.

Why creative diversity matters

Creative diversity means giving different people distinct reasons to stop, understand and act—not making twenty near-copies. Meta says Andromeda is designed to scale the growing volume of eligible creative candidates; the claim that distinct concepts help match different needs is a reasonable marketer interpretation, not a published Andromeda feature-weight rule. [1]

Concept diversity vs. cosmetic variation

  • Distinct: a 20-second spill-proof demo, a customer’s meal-prep routine, a comparison of materials, and a bundle/value offer.
  • Mostly cosmetic: the same product shot with a different background color or one synonym in the headline.
  • Strategic test: a concept has a clear audience motivation, a message, proof and an action—not only a different crop.

Creative fatigue is not a calendar rule

Meta defines creative fatigue as a situation where an audience has seen the same creative too often and may be less likely to engage, potentially increasing cost per result. Meta’s delivery status can use recent exposure and cost-per-result context. There is no universal “refresh every 14 days” rule in this guide. [21]

Watch delivery volume, frequency, thumb-stop/hold metrics, CTR and conversion efficiency together. A dip can also come from offer, seasonality, auction, site or tracking changes.

Why “more concepts” is not the same as “more active ads”
Meta’s own ad-volume help warns that running too many ads at once can reduce delivery per ad and delay learning; it also recommends fewer ads per ad set while maintaining diverse creative assets. That is why this report recommends a managed concept portfolio, not a fixed 10/20/30-ad quota. A usable creative library and active-ad count are related but not identical. [11]

12
Structure should follow the business

What Andromeda means for campaign structure

Default toward consolidation when campaigns share the same goal, event, offer, economics and constraints. Do not turn that into “one campaign and one ad set for every advertiser.” The value is concentrated spend and fewer fragmented learning units, not simplicity for its own sake.

Audience fragmentation

Splitting similar people into adjacent ad sets may reduce the number of opportunities and outcomes each set sees. Meta’s Help Center describes combining similar ad sets as a way to pool budget and learning. [10]

Budget fragmentation

If total budget is spread across many cells, some cells may not gather enough results for stable optimization. Meta says many ad sets can mean fewer learning opportunities per set. [9][10]

Learning limitations

Meta says ad sets usually exit learning after about 50 results in the week after the last significant edit. Treat this as platform guidance, not a universal promise or proof that 50 conversions is statistically sufficient for every business. [9]

Consolidate when

  • Same objective, conversion event, offer and landing path.
  • Audience splits are adjacent or created only for reporting.
  • Each ad set is underfunded or learning-limited.
  • Placement/format splits can be handled with Advantage+ placements or asset customization.

Keep separate when

  • Different business outcomes, optimization events, margins or purchase cycles.
  • Distinct countries, legal eligibility, language, product availability or service areas.
  • Different budgets/guardrails are contractually or strategically necessary.
  • A controlled A/B or holdout experiment needs isolated cells.
  • Warm and prospecting strategies need different messages/exclusions and the account can fund each.
Important trade-off: Consolidation can improve signal density but reduce independent budget control or make reporting less granular. Use breakdowns for descriptive analysis, and proper experiments for causal comparisons. Meta explicitly cautions that opportunity scores/recommendations do not guarantee future performance. [10]
13
Broader does not mean boundary-free

What Andromeda means for audience targeting

Broad targeting

A broad audience leaves more eligible people for automated delivery, usually while retaining hard constraints such as location or minimum age. It can work when the offer has broad fit, the objective is clear and the system has useful feedback. It is not guaranteed to outperform a genuinely distinct audience or a controlled test.

Use broad as a starting hypothesis when…

  • Product eligibility is broad within a defined geography.
  • One business outcome and offer apply to the whole pool.
  • You have credible event feedback and distinct creative concepts.

Advantage+ audience: controls vs. suggestions

Meta’s current help distinguishes audience controls, which limit who can see an ad, from audience suggestions, which guide AI but may expand beyond the suggestion when it predicts better results. If reaching a specific audience is mandatory, use the appropriate control setting where available. [6]

The UI and available controls vary by campaign objective, category, region and rollout. Check the live Ads Manager flow—not a screenshot from an old tutorial.

Lookalikes

A lookalike is a prospecting option based on similarity to a source audience. A valuable seed (qualified customers, not just any lead) matters. In some Advantage+ flows, audiences can be suggestions rather than a strict delivery boundary. [29][6]

Retargeting

Custom audiences can be built from website, app, catalog, customer-list and engagement sources. Retarget only where the message and business case justify it; do not assume every warm pool needs its own small ad set. [29]

Exclusions + first-party data

Use exclusions when needed to avoid serving an irrelevant offer to recent buyers, ineligible prospects or people already converted. First-party data can help seed audiences and send downstream event outcomes—but only with lawful permission, correct governance and accurate identifiers. [14][15]

Restrictions still matter. Legal requirements, Special Ad Categories, minimum age, location/serviceability, inventory and product eligibility can require audience constraints. Available controls depend on campaign and targeting mode; Meta documents, for example, that detailed-targeting expansion is distinct from location, age and gender settings. Never broaden beyond what the business is allowed or able to serve. [6][7]

Marketer rule: Do not automatically treat audience targeting as the primary optimization lever. First ask whether the audience is a true business boundary, a useful hypothesis, or merely a historic convention.

Discovery vs. proof

What Andromeda means for creative testing

Traditional A/B testing answers a controlled question. A creative portfolio gives the delivery system multiple options and helps you discover messages worth investigating. These are complementary methods, not substitutes.

A practical concept-testing sequence
Creative conceptProblem, buyer motivation, promise
→
HookFirst meaningful reason to stop
→
FormatVideo, still, carousel, catalog
→
OfferPrice, bundle, trial or guarantee
→
CTA + landing-page matchSame promise continues after the click
→
Performance signalQualified outcome—not CTR alone

Portfolio testing: learn what resonates

  • Run different concepts/angles in one comparable campaign where delivery controls allow.
  • Review the total campaign result and creative delivery breakdowns; the system chooses where to spend.
  • Use the portfolio to find candidates for deeper testing.
  • Remember: uneven delivery is optimized allocation, not an unbiased experiment.

A/B testing: answer one question

  • State a hypothesis: “A 30-day trial message produces more qualified demos than a discount message.”
  • Change one major variable where possible; keep audience, budget, event and landing experience comparable.
  • Use Meta Experiments/A-B tools rather than manually switching campaigns on/off.
  • Wait for enough volume and a full conversion cycle; a short test can be inconclusive.
Meta-documented Meta’s A/B testing guidance describes comparing two strategies with separated audience segments and recommends a hypothesis; it warns informal on/off testing can produce unreliable results. [20]

Test in layers; avoid changing everything at once

  1. Concept: Which customer problem/value proposition deserves attention?
  2. Hook: Which opening earns attention for the same core concept?
  3. Format: Does the message work better as demo, testimonial, carousel or still?
  4. Offer: Is the business proposition compelling and economically viable?
  5. CTA + landing page: Does the post-click experience deliver what the ad promised?

When the spend is too small to support multiple cells, choose fewer high-quality tests, run them sequentially, or use a lighter portfolio. Avoid simultaneously testing concept × audience × format × offer × landing page: the result may be uninterpretable.

The learning signal has to be true

Tracking and conversion signals

Conversion data quality matters because it is used for measurement and can help the delivery system optimize toward business events. A perfect retrieval model cannot repair a Purchase event that fires on a button click, a duplicate Purchase, or a lead event that never becomes a qualified customer. [12][14]

Tracking and conversion-signal flow
Website / app / CRMActual business actions and outcomes
→
Meta PixelBrowser-side web events
Conversions APIServer/platform/CRM event connection
→
Meta dataset + Events ManagerEvent receipt, matching, diagnostics, deduplication
→
Optimization + reportingDelivery feedback and attributed results

Pixel in one minute

The Meta Pixel is browser code that records website events (such as ViewContent, Lead or Purchase) and makes them available in Events Manager and campaign measurement/optimization. Events should match actions that truly happened. [13]

Browser loading errors, connectivity issues and privacy controls can affect what is received; a browser event count is not the same as a CRM order ledger. [14]

Conversions API (CAPI) in one minute

CAPI sends marketing events directly from a server, website platform, app or CRM to Meta. It can support website, app, offline and messaging event sources and can improve connectivity, matching, optimization and measurement when implemented correctly. [14]

CAPI is not a permission bypass. Respect applicable consent, privacy law, Meta policies and user choices; send only data you are authorized to share. [15]

Five checks that protect signal quality

  1. Use a truthful event: fire Purchase after confirmed purchase; use Lead only for a real submission/lead action. Standard events are predefined actions Meta supports; custom events are for actions without a standard equivalent. [12][16]
  2. Include useful parameters: value and currency for purchases, content IDs for catalog behavior, and the correct action source where relevant. [13]
  3. Use Pixel + CAPI thoughtfully: Meta recommends redundant browser/server setups for the same website events to improve coverage, with deduplication to avoid double counting. [15]
  4. Deduplicate: for the same browser/server event, align the browser event ID with the CAPI event_id and use the same event name. Meta’s developer documentation describes these as deduplication parameters. [17]
  5. Validate: check Events Manager Test Events/Diagnostics, event coverage, event match quality, freshness and deduplication feedback. Meta recommends aiming for Good/Great match quality and lawful permissions—not a magic score that guarantees ROAS. [15]
Keep event configuration distinct: (1) the campaign’s optimization event is the action you ask delivery to pursue; (2) Aggregated Event Measurement (AEM) is a separate privacy-related measurement framework; and (3) Apple SKAdNetwork has its own version-specific app-conversion configuration. Meta’s live AEM help says updated website conversion campaigns no longer require advertisers to prioritize eight events per domain, where that updated setup is available. SKAdNetwork app-event priority is a separate configuration—not the campaign optimization event and not AEM. Check current account/domain/app guidance and rollout status. [30][19]

Attribution is credit assignment, not a sales ledger

Meta Ads Manager reports events attributed to ad interactions according to settings, while Events Manager may show received events regardless of whether they were attributed. Meta explicitly notes that its reporting surfaces can show different event counts because they serve different purposes. Reconcile with your store/CRM/finance data rather than expecting every platform’s total to match. [18]

2026 measurement change to watch: Meta announced staged changes to website/in-store click-through attribution: click-through would count link clicks, while non-link social actions (such as shares/saves/likes) shift to engage-through attribution; Meta also announced shortening the engaged-video threshold from 10 to 5 seconds. Check your account’s current columns and settings before comparing periods across the change. [26]

Use three lenses: platform attribution to manage delivery under the chosen window, business/CRM reporting to validate revenue and quality, and incrementality experiments to estimate what advertising caused. Meta describes Conversion Lift as a test/holdout approach and calls these experiments a strong measurement method. Meta also offers an Incremental Attribution model, which is a separate attribution/delivery approach—not the same thing as a randomized holdout. Ordinary attribution alone does not prove causation. [26][27][28]

Deduplication example
One real order fires a browser Purchase and a server Purchase. If both copies share a consistent event identifier and event name, Meta can recognize them as two reports of one event rather than two purchases. If the event names or identifiers mismatch, the event may not deduplicate as intended. Confirm behavior in Events Manager; don’t assume an integration is working because the dashboard shows activity. [17][15]

Machine Learning

Systems learn patterns from examples and use them to make predictions or choices rather than relying only on hand-written rules.

Marketing: delivery can adapt from available feedback, but it still needs the right objective and trustworthy event inputs.

Recommendation System

A system that finds and orders items likely to fit a person and moment.

Marketing: Andromeda is described as part of Meta’s ads recommendation system, not the entirety of every recommender on Meta apps.

Candidate Generation

An early step that creates a smaller set of options for deeper evaluation; often overlaps with “retrieval.”

Marketing: eligible ads enter a potential pool; not all candidates are ultimately delivered.

Retrieval

Fast selection of a relevant shortlist from a very large candidate set.

Marketing: this is Andromeda’s stated role. It is not the final auction winner.

Ranking

Ordering candidate ads according to predicted relevance/value for an opportunity.

Marketing: a downstream stage follows retrieval; ranking details and scores are not exposed.

Prediction

An estimate of an outcome from available inputs.

Marketing: Meta publicly describes estimated action rates; a prediction is not a promise of conversion.

Inference

Using a trained model to make a live estimate at serving time.

Marketing: Meta optimizes this for the low latency needed to choose ads quickly.

Model Training

Learning model parameters from historical or newly collected examples.

Marketing: clean, accurate conversion events are more useful than false or duplicated events.

Features

Represented inputs a model can use, such as event history, ad properties or context.

Marketing: the exact feature set is proprietary; avoid assuming that any specific creative cue controls delivery.

Signals

Evidence that indicates what happened or may happen—interactions, settings, events and context.

Marketing: set the objective and event to match real business value; follow privacy requirements.

Embeddings

Learned numeric representations that encode useful relationships between people, ads, products or events.

Marketing: explains how a system can relate items beyond a marketer’s manual interest labels; not a human-like understanding claim.

Conversion Event

A tracked action such as Purchase, Lead, CompleteRegistration or a valid custom event.

Marketing: make sure the event fires only when the real action occurs.

Optimization Event

The outcome an ad set/campaign tells the delivery system to pursue.

Marketing: choose the deepest truthful and supportable signal—not a convenient but misleading proxy.

Attribution

A rule/model that assigns credit to an ad interaction within a selected window.

Marketing: useful for platform reporting; not by itself a causal lift test or revenue ledger.

Feedback Loop

Observed results inform later predictions, delivery decisions or model updates.

Marketing: broken or misleading tracking feeds weak evidence into measurement and optimization.

Exploration

Trying less-certain options to learn about them.

Marketing: new concepts need room to receive delivery; this is a general concept, not an Andromeda setting.

Exploitation

Favoring options with stronger known predicted performance.

Marketing: a system may spend more on options it predicts will work; don’t assume equal testing without an A/B experiment.

Creative Fatigue

Repeated exposure to the same creative can reduce engagement and raise cost per result.

Marketing: diagnose with exposure and performance trends, then refresh the concept if needed.

Audience Fragmentation

Splitting a similar audience/budget across too many small delivery units.

Marketing: combine comparable ad sets where it makes business sense; Meta says fragmentation can reduce learning opportunities per set.

Auction

The process used to select an ad for an impression opportunity.

Marketing: bid is one factor; Meta describes estimated action rate and ad quality as other major factors.

Meta Pixel

Browser-side code that shares website events with Meta.

Marketing: QA event timing, names, values and duplicate installs.

Conversions API (CAPI)

A direct connection for marketing events from a server/platform/CRM to Meta.

Marketing: use to improve connectivity and event feedback where appropriate; not a privacy bypass.

Event Match Quality

A Meta indicator of how effectively customer-information parameters may match server events to Meta accounts.

Marketing: review the trend and missing parameters; a strong score does not guarantee campaign performance.

Aggregated Event Measurement (AEM)

A Meta privacy-related framework for measuring web and app events in eligible contexts.

Marketing: Meta's live guide says updated website AEM no longer requires the old eight-event priority setup; app/SKAdNetwork event configuration is separate. [30]

Incrementality

The additional outcome caused by advertising beyond what would have happened without it.

Marketing: estimate with a holdout/Conversion Lift or other sound experiment, not attribution alone.

Separate fact from hype

Myths vs. reality

Myth: “Andromeda completely replaces targeting.”

Reality: It is a retrieval engine in ads recommendation. Campaign eligibility and audience controls remain important; some settings are suggestions while others restrict reach. [1][6]

Myth: “Audiences no longer matter.”

Reality: Location, eligibility, exclusions and policy can define the valid pool. Audience hypotheses can still be useful when tested for a real business question.

Myth: “More ads always produce better performance.”

Reality: Meta warns that too many active ads can dilute delivery and learning. Aim for meaningful concepts at a volume your budget can support. [11]

Myth: “Broad targeting always wins.”

Reality: Broad can increase opportunity but can also be inappropriate for constrained products, narrow service areas or distinct economics. Test under the real objective.

Myth: “Andromeda reads the ad like a human.”

Reality: Meta describes neural retrieval, features and embeddings, but the public articles do not establish human-like understanding or disclose exact content encoders. [1]

Myth: “Andromeda guarantees higher CTR or ROAS.”

Reality: Meta reported retrieval recall/ad-quality improvements on selected segments. That does not promise any advertiser’s CTR, conversion rate, CPA or ROAS. [1]

Myth: “Creative quantity is the main key.”

Reality: Variety, truth, craft, offer fit and adequate delivery matter. Twenty versions of one message may be less useful than a smaller, well-designed portfolio.

Myth: “The algorithm can compensate for bad tracking.”

Reality: Poorly defined or duplicated events weaken reporting and can teach the wrong lesson. Fix signal health first. [15][17]

Myth: “Advantage+ and Andromeda are the same thing.”

Reality: Advantage+ is a suite of advertiser-facing automation products; Andromeda is a backend retrieval system. One can support the other without being synonymous. [1][8]

Myth: “Retrieval is the auction.”

Reality: Retrieval shortlists candidates. Ranking/prediction and auction/delivery perform distinct jobs; the exact production boundary is not fully public. [1][4]

The 30-second picture

Final one-page mental model

An advertiser defines the business outcome and eligibility rules, supplies ads and trustworthy event feedback; Meta retrieves and ranks eligible candidates, runs its auction/delivery process, observes available outcomes, and updates future estimates. The marketer remains accountable for whether the outcome is profitable and incremental.

System question

“Among eligible ads, which options are plausible and valuable for this impression?”

Marketer question

“Does the ad create qualified, incremental, profitable business outcomes?”

Operational answer

Give the system clear goals, valid boundaries, diverse creative and truthful feedback—then measure beyond one dashboard.

Keep this sentence: Andromeda improves how Meta retrieves ads from a huge candidate pool; it does not make audience rules, creative strategy, conversion tracking or measurement judgment obsolete.
